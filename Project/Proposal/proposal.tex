\begin{abstract}
DreamerV3 learns a world model from experience and then trains its policy entirely inside that model---it learns by dreaming rather than by doing. This approach works across dozens of domains without changing a single hyperparameter \citep{hafner2025dreamerv3}. But how accurate are those dreams? If the world model gets something wrong, how does the agent still succeed? And does imagination accuracy even matter for performance? This work tests this by training DreamerV3 on Snake---a game with simple, deterministic dynamics that make imagination errors easy to measure. By extracting imagined trajectories at multiple training checkpoints and comparing them against real game states, this analysis quantifies what the world model gets right, what it gets wrong, and whether imagination accuracy actually correlates with game performance. Snake has not been studied with DreamerV3 before, and no prior work has systematically measured imagination fidelity in this way.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Model-based reinforcement learning takes a fundamentally different approach from model-free methods. Instead of learning a policy directly from environment interactions, model-based agents first learn how the environment works and then use that learned model to plan \citep{sutton2018rl}. The Dreamer family of algorithms takes this idea further---the agent trains its policy entirely inside the learned world model, never touching the real environment during policy optimization \citep{hafner2020dreamerv1, hafner2021dreamerv2, hafner2025dreamerv3}.

DreamerV3 represents the current state of the art in this approach. It masters Atari games, continuous control tasks, and even collects diamonds in Minecraft, all with the same fixed hyperparameters \citep{hafner2025dreamerv3}. However, most research on DreamerV3 focuses on end-task performance---how high the score is, how fast the agent learns. What gets less attention is the world model itself. The agent is training inside an imagined version of the environment. So how faithful is that imagination to reality?

This is not just an academic question. If the world model predicts the wrong outcome for an action, the agent is optimizing for a scenario that does not exist. Understanding when and how imagination diverges from reality could reveal fundamental insights about why model-based RL works as well as it does---and where it breaks down.

The central question is: \textbf{Does DreamerV3 need accurate imagination to learn effective policies, or can it succeed even when its world model is wrong?} This decomposes into three testable hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item[\textbf{H1:}] The world model's imagination gets more accurate as training goes on---it learns to dream better over time.
    \item[\textbf{H2:}] Better imagination leads to better play. If the dreams are more accurate, the agent scores higher.
    \item[\textbf{H3:}] Getting the game state right matters more than getting the pixels right. Knowing where the snake and food are is more important than producing a sharp image.
\end{itemize}
If H2 holds, imagination quality is a bottleneck---improve the world model and the policy improves with it. But if H2 is weak or flat-out wrong, that means the world model is capturing the right structure for making decisions without actually being a faithful picture of reality. That would go a long way toward explaining why DreamerV3 works across so many different domains without changing anything.

This work investigates the question by training DreamerV3 on Snake. Snake is an ideal testbed for imagination analysis because its dynamics are simple and deterministic: the snake moves one cell per step in the current direction, food appears at random locations, and the game ends on collision with a wall or the snake's own body. This simplicity makes it possible to precisely identify what the world model gets right and what it gets wrong. There is no visual clutter, no stochastic physics, no ambiguity. When the model imagines a trajectory, each frame can be compared directly against what would actually happen.

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A systematic analysis of DreamerV3's imagination accuracy on Snake, measuring how well imagined trajectories match real game states across training
    \item Evidence on whether imagination accuracy correlates with task performance---does the agent need accurate dreams to play well?
    \item An element-level breakdown of what the world model learns to predict (snake position, food location, collisions) and what it struggles with
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}

\subsection{World Models in Reinforcement Learning}

The idea of learning a model of the environment and using it for planning has a long history in RL \citep{sutton2018rl}. \citet{ha2018world} demonstrated that an agent could learn a compressed representation of its environment using a variational autoencoder and a recurrent network, then train a policy entirely within that learned model. This showed that ``dreaming'' about an environment could be enough to learn effective behavior.

\citet{hafner2019planet} formalized this with the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), which maintains both a deterministic recurrent state and a stochastic latent state. The deterministic path captures persistent information across time steps, while the stochastic path models uncertainty about the environment. This architecture became the backbone of all subsequent Dreamer agents.

\subsection{The Dreamer Family}

DreamerV1 introduced the idea of training actor-critic policies entirely within imagined trajectories generated by the RSSM \citep{hafner2020dreamerv1}. DreamerV2 improved this with discrete latent representations, replacing the Gaussian latents with categorical variables, which produced sharper world model predictions and achieved human-level performance on the Atari benchmark \citep{hafner2021dreamerv2}.

DreamerV3 further refined the approach with symlog predictions for handling varying reward scales and a fixed set of hyperparameters that work across diverse domains \citep{hafner2025dreamerv3}. It was the first single algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch---a task requiring long-horizon planning across a complex open world. The key to DreamerV3's generality is that it treats world modeling as a separate problem from policy optimization, letting the same architecture adapt to radically different environments.

\subsection{Related Work on World Model Analysis}

Several works have extended or modified the Dreamer architecture. \citet{deng2022dreamerpro} removed the reconstruction objective entirely, training the world model with prototypical representations instead. \citet{zhang2023oderssm} extended the RSSM to handle irregularly sampled data using ordinary differential equations. \citet{sekar2020plan2explore} used the world model for self-supervised exploration without task rewards. \citet{lin2024dynalang} incorporated language into the world model, enabling agents to use instructions and descriptions for planning.

However, these works focus on improving the world model or the policy---not on measuring how accurate the imagination actually is. \citet{walker2023modelbased} investigated model-based learning's role in exploration and transfer, and \citet{sullivan2023rewardscale} analyzed whether DreamerV3's design choices transfer to model-free methods. But a direct, systematic measurement of imagination fidelity---comparing dreamed trajectories against ground truth---remains unexplored.

\subsection{Snake as an MDP}

Snake is formalized as a Markov Decision Process $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, P, R, \gamma)$:

\textbf{State Space} $\mathcal{S}$: Each state is a grid of size $N \times N$ containing the snake body segments, the head position, the current direction, and the food location. States are rendered as RGB images for DreamerV3's visual encoder.

\textbf{Action Space} $\mathcal{A}$: Four discrete actions---up, down, left, right. The snake cannot reverse direction (moving left while heading right has no effect).

\textbf{Transition Function} $P(s'|s,a)$: Transitions are deterministic given the current state and action. The snake moves one cell in the chosen direction. If it eats food, it grows by one segment and new food spawns at a random empty cell. The only stochasticity is food placement.

\textbf{Reward Function} $R(s,a,s')$: $+1$ for eating food, $-1$ for collision (game over), $0$ otherwise.

\textbf{Discount Factor} $\gamma$: Set to 0.999 to encourage long-horizon survival and food collection.

The simplicity of these dynamics is precisely why Snake is a good choice for imagination analysis. Every transition except food spawning is fully predictable, making it possible to measure exactly where the world model's predictions diverge from reality.

\section{Proposed Methods}

\subsection{Training DreamerV3 on Snake}

A Gymnasium-compatible Snake environment will be implemented and used to train DreamerV3 with the publicly available codebase. The environment renders the game as a small RGB image (e.g., 64$\times$64 pixels) with distinct colors for the snake head, body, food, and background. Training uses a standard grid size (e.g., 10$\times$10), and performance data is collected over the full training run.

\subsection{Extracting Imagined Trajectories}

At regular checkpoints during training (e.g., every 50,000 environment steps), the following procedure is applied:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Record a real trajectory by running the current policy in the actual environment
    \item Feed the initial observation from that trajectory into the world model
    \item Roll out the world model using the same sequence of actions, generating an imagined trajectory
    \item Compare the two trajectories frame by frame
\end{enumerate}

This gives us matched pairs of real and imagined game states at each checkpoint, using identical action sequences so the only difference is the world model's prediction accuracy.

\subsection{Measuring Imagination Accuracy}

Accuracy is evaluated at two levels:

\textbf{Frame-level metrics:} Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) between imagined and real frames. These capture overall visual fidelity of the world model's predictions.

\textbf{Semantic metrics:} Since Snake's state is fully interpretable, game-specific information can be extracted from both real and imagined frames:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Snake head position error (Euclidean distance)
    \item Snake body segment accuracy (percentage of correctly placed segments)
    \item Food location accuracy (correct or not)
    \item Collision prediction accuracy (does the model correctly predict game-over states?)
\end{itemize}

These semantic metrics go beyond pixel similarity. The world model might produce slightly blurry images (low SSIM) while still correctly representing the snake's position and the food location. Conversely, it might produce sharp images that place the snake in the wrong spot. Separating these reveals what the model actually learns.

\subsection{Correlation Analysis}

The analysis correlates imagination accuracy with game performance across checkpoints. If the agent's score improves while imagination accuracy stays flat, that suggests the policy can succeed despite an imperfect world model. If accuracy and performance track together, that suggests accurate imagination is critical. The analysis also examines whether specific semantic metrics (e.g., food location accuracy) are more predictive of performance than others.

\section{Expected Challenges}

\textbf{Environment integration:} DreamerV3 expects Gymnasium-compatible environments with image observations. Building a Snake environment that renders clean, consistent frames at the right resolution will require careful implementation.

\textbf{Trajectory alignment:} Imagined trajectories may diverge from real ones after just a few steps, especially early in training when the world model is poor. The analysis must handle cases where the imagined game ends at a different time step than the real one.

\textbf{Semantic extraction:} Pulling game-state information (snake position, food location) from rendered frames requires either access to the underlying game state during real play or a reliable extraction method from pixels. The approach is to log the true game state alongside rendered frames to avoid this issue.

\textbf{Computational cost:} DreamerV3 training is GPU-intensive. The available hardware is an RTX 5070 Ti with 16GB VRAM running PyTorch 2.7.0 with CUDA 12.8, which should be sufficient for a 64$\times$64 observation space.

\section{Conclusion}

DreamerV3 succeeds by learning to imagine its environment and training inside that imagination. But how accurate those imaginations are---and whether accuracy even matters for performance---remains an open question. By applying DreamerV3 to Snake, a game simple enough to make imagination errors precisely measurable, this work provides the first systematic analysis of world model fidelity in the Dreamer framework. The results will reveal what DreamerV3's world model actually learns, how its imagination evolves during training, and whether better dreams lead to better play.
